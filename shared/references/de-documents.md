# German Application Documents

What a German employer actually expects to receive. This is the part US-centric job tools get wrong: a beautifully ATS-optimized one-page American resume with a bulleted "Professional Summary" and no attachments reads, to a German recruiter, as an incomplete application.

Applies whenever `language.md` says **Deutsch**. For **English** applications at international employers, use normal CV/cover-letter conventions — but the Bewerbungsmappe assembly rules at the bottom still apply if the employer asks for one PDF.

---

## 1. Anschreiben (DIN 5008 letter)

**One page. Never two.** The Anschreiben is a formal business letter, and its layout is standardized by DIN 5008. Recruiters at traditional employers notice when it is wrong.

### Required elements, in order

| # | Element | Rule |
|---|---------|------|
| 1 | **Absender** — your name, street, PLZ + Ort, phone, email | Top of page. |
| 2 | **Empfänger** — company, department, contact person, street, PLZ + Ort | Left-aligned block. Use the named contact from the ad if there is one. |
| 3 | **Ort, Datum** | Right-aligned. `Berlin, 12. März 2026` or `12.03.2026`. |
| 4 | **Betreff** | Bold. **No "Betreff:" label** — writing the word is obsolete and dates you. Must contain the job title *exactly as advertised, including `(m/w/d)`*, plus the **Kennziffer / Referenznummer** if the ad gives one. |
| 5 | **Anrede** | Named person: `Sehr geehrte Frau Müller,` / `Sehr geehrter Herr Dr. Schmidt,`. No name findable: `Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,`. Startup using "du": `Hallo Frau Müller,`. |
| 6 | **Body** | 3–4 short paragraphs. See structure below. |
| 7 | **Grußformel** | `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` — **no comma after it.** |
| 8 | **Signature** | Blank space for a signature, then your typed name. |
| 9 | **Anlagen** | The word `Anlagen`, then the list: Lebenslauf, Arbeitszeugnisse, Abschlusszeugnis. |

### The lowercase rule

The Anrede ends in a comma, so the sentence continues — **the first word of the body is lowercase** unless it is a noun or "Ich":

```
Sehr geehrte Frau Müller,

mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Stellenanzeige gelesen.
```

Not `Mit großem Interesse`. This single character is one of the fastest tells that a letter was written by a non-native speaker or a translation tool. Get it right.

### Body structure

1. **Opening** — why *this* role at *this* company. Never "hiermit bewerbe ich mich auf die ausgeschriebene Stelle" (says nothing, marks a template). Reference something specific: their product, a project, a market position.
2. **Evidence** — 1–2 paragraphs mapping your actual experience to their stated requirements. Quote their language where it is honest to do so.
3. **Fit / motivation** — why you, why them, briefly.
4. **Close** — the practical block German recruiters look for:
   - **Gehaltsvorstellung** if the ad asks: `Meine Gehaltsvorstellung liegt bei 75.000 EUR brutto p.a.` Always annual gross. Never monthly, never net.
   - **Eintrittstermin** — realistic, accounting for your Kündigungsfrist: `Verfügbar bin ich ab dem 1. Juli 2026.`
   - Standard closer: `Über eine Einladung zu einem persönlichen Gespräch freue ich mich.`

If the ad asks for Gehaltsvorstellung and you omit it, some employers filter the application out. If it does not ask, do not volunteer it.

### Typography

A4, 1 page, 10–12pt, a normal business face (Arial, Helvetica, Times, Calibri). DIN 5008 conventions: left margin ~25mm, right ~20mm, single line spacing, address block positioned so the letter would fit a window envelope. Exact millimetre values matter only for printed post; hold the *order and content* rules strictly and the typography loosely.

## 2. Lebenslauf (tabellarisch)

The German CV is **tabular and reverse-chronological** — a two-column layout of dates against content. It is not a narrative document. There is no "Summary" or "Objective" section, and no "References available upon request" (you attach the actual references instead).

### Sections, in order

| Section | Contents |
|---------|----------|
| **Persönliche Daten** | Name, address, phone, email. Optional and increasingly omitted: Geburtsdatum, Geburtsort, Staatsangehörigkeit. **Omit Familienstand and religion** — no employer needs them. |
| **Berufserfahrung** | Newest first. `MM/JJJJ – MM/JJJJ`, then Position, Company, Ort, then 2–5 bullets. |
| **Ausbildung** | Newest first. Degree, institution, and **the grade** (`Abschlussnote: 1,3`). Germans include grades; Americans do not. Include it if it is good. |
| **Kenntnisse** | **Sprachen with CEFR levels** (see below) and technical skills. |
| **Weiterbildungen / Zertifikate** | Courses, certifications, with dates. |
| **Ehrenamt / Interessen** | Optional, two lines maximum. Genuine engagement only. |
| **Ort, Datum + Unterschrift** | Traditional signed footer. Expected at conservative employers, optional at startups. |

**Length: 1–2 pages, hard maximum 2.**

### Language levels — state them, and state them honestly

German CVs list languages with a level. Use CEFR, or the conventional German wording:

| CEFR | Conventional German label |
|------|---------------------------|
| A1–A2 | Grundkenntnisse |
| B1–B2 | Gute Kenntnisse / fließend |
| C1 | Verhandlungssicher |
| C2 | Verhandlungssicher / annähernd Muttersprache |
| native | Muttersprache |

Take the level from the candidate's `profile.md`. **Never inflate it.** An overstated level is checked in the first phone call, in German, without warning. See `shared/references/language-decision.md`.

### Gaps

Germany scrutinizes **Lücken im Lebenslauf** far more than the US does. An unexplained gap over ~3 months invites suspicion. Label it plainly and truthfully — `Elternzeit`, `Weiterbildung`, `Sabbatical`, `Umzug nach Deutschland`, `Arbeitssuche`. A named gap is a non-event; an unexplained one is a question in the interview. Never fabricate an entry to paper one over, and never adjust dates to close one.

### Photo (Bewerbungsfoto)

Legally optional since the AGG (2006), and no employer may require one. In practice:

| Employer | Photo |
|----------|-------|
| Mittelstand, family-owned, public sector, Kanzlei, Klinik | Still commonly expected |
| DAX corporates | Neutral — usually included |
| Startups, international tech, research institutes | Omit; often explicitly discouraged |

Professional headshot, top right, roughly 35×45mm. **Ask the user once during setup, store the answer, and never re-ask per job.** If they have no professional photo, recommend omitting rather than using a cropped holiday snapshot.

## 3. Bewerbungsmappe (the assembled application)

German employers frequently want **one PDF containing everything**, in a fixed order. Sending three loose files when the portal asked for a Bewerbungsmappe is a real, avoidable mistake.

### Order inside the PDF

1. **Deckblatt** (optional) — position, name, contact, photo
2. **Anschreiben**
3. **Lebenslauf**
4. **Motivationsschreiben** (only if explicitly requested — rare outside academia and scholarships)
5. **Arbeitszeugnisse** — newest first
6. **Abschlusszeugnisse** — degree certificates, then relevant Zertifikate

### Practical rules

- **File name**: `Bewerbung_Nachname_Vorname_Position.pdf`. Not `resume_final_v3.pdf`.
- **Size**: many portals cap at 5 MB total. Compress scans; do not send 40 MB of 600-dpi TIFFs.
- **Format**: PDF only. Never .docx, never .pages, never a zip.
- **Email applications**: subject line = the Betreff (`Bewerbung als Software Engineer (m/w/d), Kennziffer 4711`). Body is 3–4 polite sentences pointing at the attachment; the real letter is the attached Anschreiben, not the email body.
- **Zeugnisse the candidate does not have**: never invent one. Write `Zeugnisse werden auf Wunsch nachgereicht` in the Anlagen line.

Before assembling, run `/paperwork:zeugnis` over any Arbeitszeugnis the candidate plans to attach. A German reference letter can be politely worded and still say something damaging in code — see `shared/references/zeugnis-code.md`. Attaching one unread is a self-inflicted wound.
